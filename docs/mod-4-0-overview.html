14  Module 4 Overview

14.1 Introduction

Welcome to Module 4 of ECON 706.

Module 4 focuses on evaluating the performance of Ordinary Least Squares (OLS) regressions by examining measures of the “Goodness of Fit.” The primary objective is to move beyond the simple minimization of squared residuals, which is a poor measure for comparing different models due to its sensitivity to scaling. Students are introduced to the M0 matrix as a tool to calculate total variation and the coefficient of determination (\(R^2\)), which measures the proportion of variation in the dependent variable explained by the regression model.

The module also addresses the limitations of \(R^2\), specifically its tendency to never decrease as more variables are added to a model, regardless of their statistical significance. To provide a more accurate assessment, students learn about Adjusted \(R^2\), which penalizes the inclusion of unnecessary variables. Through practical applications in R, students explore how to manually calculate these matrices and interpret standard statistical output to determine if adding new variables truly improves a model’s explanatory power.

14.2 Learning Objectives

By the end of this module, you should be able to:

  • MLO 1: Explain the role of the M0 matrix in measuring the total variation of a dependent variable within a regression framework.
  • MLO 2: Calculate and interpret the coefficient of determination ($R^2$) to evaluate the explained variation of a least squares regression.
  • MLO 3: Differentiate between \(R^2\) and Adjusted \(R^2\), specifically regarding how each metric responds to the addition of statistically insignificant variables.
  • MLO 4: Analyze standard statistical output to determine if the inclusion of a specific variable significantly improves the model’s goodness of fit.
  • MLO 5: Implement matrix calculations in R to derive the Total Sum of Squares (SST) and verify results against standard regression packages.
